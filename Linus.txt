Linus Torvalds is a Finnish software engineer best known for creating Linux.
He was born in 1969 in Helsinki and got interested in computers early, mostly by tinkering and teaching himself how things work.
While studying computer science in the early 1990s, he started writing his own operating system kernel as a personal project,
mainly because he wanted something better than what was available to him at the time.

That small side project grew into Linux, which he released on the internet in 1991.
Instead of keeping control, Linus encouraged others to contribute, fix bugs, and improve it.
This approach helped Linux grow fast and become the foundation for servers, supercomputers, Android phones, and much of the internet.
He later also created Git, a version control system now used by millions of developers.

Linus is known for being extremely direct, and at times blunt or harsh, when discussing code and technical decisions.
Some people admire his honesty and clarity; others criticize his communication style as too harsh.
Over time he has acknowledged this and adjusted how he interacts with the community.
Today, he still oversees Linux development, acting as a final decision-maker rather than a day-to-day coder.

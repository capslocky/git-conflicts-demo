Linus Torvalds is a Finnish programmer who became famous for starting Linux.
He was born in 1969 in Helsinki and grew up as a curious kid who liked figuring out how computers actually worked.
In the early 1990s, while he was a university student,
he began hacking on a simple operating system kernel mostly for fun and learning,without any big plan for it to change the world.

What started as a simple side project eventually became Linux, and he put it online in 1991.
Instead of keeping control, Linus encouraged others to contribute, fix bugs, and improve it.
This approach helped Linux grow fast and become the foundation for servers, supercomputers, Android phones, and much of the internet.
He later also created Git, a version control system now used by millions of developers.

He has a reputation for being very blunt, especially when giving feedback on code and design choices.
Some people admire his honesty and clarity; others criticize his communication style as too harsh.
Over time he has acknowledged this and adjusted how he interacts with the community.
Today, he still oversees Linux development, acting as a final decision-maker rather than a day-to-day coder.

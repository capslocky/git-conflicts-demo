Dennis Ritchie was an American computer scientist best known for creating the C programming language and co-developing Unix.
Working at Bell Labs in the late 1960s and 1970s, he focused on making systems that were simple, fast, and portable,
which ended up shaping how modern operating systems and software are built.
C and Unix became the foundation for much of today’s computing, influencing everything from Linux and macOS to embedded systems.
Unlike many tech celebrities, Ritchie stayed low-key and avoided hype, but his impact on computing is hard to overstate.
